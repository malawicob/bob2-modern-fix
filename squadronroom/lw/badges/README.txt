pilot-badge.png is the Flugzeugfuehrerabzeichen, the pilot's badge, and
it is the 1957 pattern rather than the wartime one. That was Patrick's
choice of source and it is the right one for something downloaded from a
public page: the 1957 pattern is the version the Federal Republic
sanctioned for wear, with no swastika on it, and it is what most
published reference works print.

It arrived as a dark line engraving on white paper and needed three
things doing to it.

The caption, the word Flugzeugfuehrerabzeichen printed underneath, was
cut off by finding the band of clear white between it and the badge.
Left in, it would have appeared as a line of floating text on the tunic.

The white was made transparent and the drawing INVERTED. The Room's
tunic is nearly black and a dark engraving on it is close to invisible;
inverted it reads as the pale metal badge it actually is.

The mid greys were lifted afterwards. At the first setting the engraving's
shading went muddy against the panel and the badge read as a smudge
rather than as metal.

It is shown at 62 pixels where the RAF wings are shown at 52, because
this badge is taller than it is wide and the wings are the opposite, so
at equal height it looks the smaller thing.

The collar patches beside it are drawn by dev/make_lw_badges.py. See the
note at the top of that script for what the references do and do not
settle, and why nothing here is a copied image.

ek2.png, ek1.png and ritterkreuz.png are the Iron Cross ladder, all 1957
patterns like the pilot badge, so none of them carries a swastika. Their
white backgrounds were flooded out from the edges rather than cleared by
colour: the crosses have pale silver beading and the EK II ribbon has
white stripes, and a colour-based clear would have eaten through both.

A fourth image was supplied and is NOT used. It is the Winterschlacht im
Osten medal, the Eastern Front winter medal of 1941/42, instituted 26 May
1942. A man flying the Channel in 1940 could not have had it. It is left
out for the same reason the RAF ladder leaves out the CGM (Flying), which
was not instituted until November 1942.
