Version 1.8.3 is up.

The main thing this time is the menus. If you have ever had the briefing
text sitting on top of the tab row, or the view coming up far too big
once you are flying, it turns out those were the same problem wearing
two hats. Bob.exe carries a manifest inside it that says nothing at all
about how the game wants to be scaled, so Windows makes its own mind up,
and on a high resolution screen it decides wrongly. It also writes that
decision back in the moment the game starts, which is why taking the
setting off by hand never stuck for more than one launch.

The fix puts a small manifest file next to Bob.exe that states plainly
the game does not do its own scaling, and it tells dgVoodoo to render
the desktop the game is actually handed rather than the full size of
your panel. Both halves are needed. With only the first you get correct
menus and a cockpit half again too large. Menus and cockpit now come out
right together, and it holds across a flight, which is where every
earlier attempt came apart.

The other half of the release is the Squadron Room. The sector map has
been redrawn so that forty one squadrons no longer sit on top of each
other in the London approaches. Each airfield now carries one plaque
with its squadrons on it, and the crowded ones are docked out over the
North Sea and the Channel with a line back to the field. Point at a
squadron number and you get its card, meaning the aeroplane it flew, its
station and sector, what the order of battle made of it, and the three
highest scorers on its strength that morning.

You can also now join a squadron as a sergeant pilot or with a
commission, which is not just flavour because it decides whether you are
put up for the DFM or the DFC, exactly as the RAF did it. The Victoria
Cross is in, along with a Bar to the DSO, and the Polish and Czech
squadrons are decorated by their own governments as well. A new career
starts empty now instead of inheriting the previous pilot's war, and the
Polish and Czech names in the rosters come out properly rather than as
mangled characters.

There is a new option in the setup, number 13, that turns on
antialiasing, anisotropic filtering and ReShade together. It is off
unless you ask for it, and pressing it again takes it back off. If you
are on older hardware I would try the antialiasing on its own first and
leave ReShade until you know what the rest is costing you.

One thing I should be straight about. The fix sets a Windows setting
called PreferExternalManifest, which is machine wide rather than just
for this game. It makes Windows read a manifest file sitting next to a
program in preference to the one built into it. Almost nothing else on a
normal machine ships a loose manifest file, so in practice it affects
BOB2 and nothing else, but it is the most invasive thing this mod has
ever done to a system and you should know it is there. It comes straight
back out if you remove it.
